Why Syracuse is a good match for Srinivas's interests and goals

Why Syracuse — Final Version

Orderly started as a way to stop losing track of my own assignments, and it only became something real once I had to explain it to people who had no reason to care — judges at Blue Ocean, panelists at BIZWORLD, evaluators at the Conrad Challenge. Each time, I learned the same lesson: an idea only counts once someone outside your own head has tested it. That instinct is why I'm drawn to Whitman's actual Entrepreneurship major, not just its clubs — a structured path to keep building rather than a side interest I have to justify around a different degree.

Whitman backs that major with real infrastructure: Blackstone LaunchPad has run over 6,000 students' ideas through its annual Ideas Fest pitch competition, and the Couri Hatchery pairs mentorship and workspace with actual funding through the Orange Tank and Panasci competitions. The Business Analytics major would let me formalize the AI/ML background from my coding certification inside the same school, instead of splitting it off into a separate department.

What pulled me past the campus itself is downtown Syracuse. INSPYRE, formerly the Tech Garden, is New York State's largest business incubator, mid-way through a $32 million expansion adding space for 35 more resident startups. The Syracuse Surge Accelerator runs there too, built specifically to support founders the tech industry doesn't always make room for. That's not a campus program described in a brochure — it's a functioning local economy I could plug into the same way I'm already learning to sell fintech products at The Founders' Pool.

I don't want a business degree that stays inside a classroom building. Syracuse is the rare school where the entrepreneurship major, the specific competitions, and the city's own tech incubator all point in the same direction — exactly the kind of overlap I've been building toward since Orderly was just an idea I couldn't explain yet.
